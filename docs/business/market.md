# Market: competitors, research and the business assessed (2026-10-15)

The competitive landscape, market figures and an honest view of the business. Mostly for the owner; Claude reads it only for positioning or copy work. Figures come from research on 2026-10-15: check again before any public claim.

## Competitors

- **Jira Plans + Program board** (Atlassian, Premium/Enterprise only): cross-team plans, dependencies, a "Review changes" step before writing to Jira; capacity set manually (velocity shown separately); limited to two active program boards per plan. Atlassian states it is not designed for real-time collaborative sessions such as big-room planning and recommends a whiteboard alongside it.
- **BigPicture** (Appfire): full portfolio suite, about 10,500 installs, 3.9/5; reviews cite slowness on large data, a steep learning curve and complex setup; capacity from availability plans, not history. Not "Runs on Atlassian".
- **Easy Agile Programs:** the closest pure PI-planning-in-Jira app; 4.9/5 but about 700 installs after nearly eight years; capacity entered manually; free up to 10 users. Evidence that this niche reaches hundreds of installs, not thousands.
- **piplanning.io** (owned by Scaled Agile, Inc.): external SaaS with real-time boards and sync to Jira, Azure DevOps and Rally; priced per team ($875–$1,400 per team per year).
- **Miro (Planner for Jira) and Mural:** best free-canvas collaboration; Miro's Planner drags Jira cards into sprints with two-way sync but needs Business/Enterprise plans and supports one Jira board. Bending Spoons agreed to acquire Miro (September 2026, closing expected Q4 2026): renewals in 2027 may be re-evaluated.
- **Jira Align and Atlassian's Strategy Collection (Focus, Talent, Align):** enterprise portfolio and OKRs; Focus is adding strategic planning against delivery work and capacity. Atlassian is investing in the top of OV's long-term vision, so the OKR/portfolio layer should integrate with it rather than compete.
- **Long tail:** Ativo Programs, Kvasar, Kosto, bulk sprint creation tools.
- **Forecasting and metrics apps:** ActionableAgile (Monte Carlo, about 1,400 installs), Monte Carlo gadgets, Great Gadgets, Agile Reports, Screenful: reports after the fact, not at the moment of commitment.
- **Rovo** (Atlassian AI): readiness checks and sprint summaries; no capacity-based sprint scoping today. Expect this to change within 12–24 months; OV's forecasting must stay explainable and auditable.

Where OV stands out: throughput-based capacity, probability at commitment, data health, and "Runs on Atlassian". Everything else (drag and drop, parking lot, write-back) is table stakes; dependencies, risks (ROAM) and PI objectives are expected by SAFe users and missing from the MVP.

### Competitor details

- **Easy Agile Programs:** on the Marketplace since 2018; Platinum Marketplace Partner and Scaled Agile Platform Partner; Cloud Fortified; migrating from Connect to Forge in 2026.
- **BigPicture:** install counts conflict between sources (about 10,500 on the live listing, 14,928 in one third-party guide); Cloud-only rating 3.4/5; Atlassian Enterprise Certified; its PI planning whiteboard template relies on a separate Whiteboards for Jira app.
- **Miro:**
  - Planner for Jira: dragging canvas cards into Planner not fully supported; the Jira Cards app is rated 3.4/5.
  - Markets "7 hours saved per planning session".
  - About $600M ARR, nearly 90% from business and enterprise customers; more than 750 customers paying over $100k a year.
  - The Bending Spoons acquisition values it about 92% below its 2022 valuation; Bending Spoons has a record of deep layoffs after acquisitions (WeTransfer, Evernote).
- **Jira Align:** third-party reports cite about $27,000 a year at entry and about $77,400 a year for 1–50 users through the Strategy Collection (not an official price list).
- **ActionableAgile:** about $22 per user per month standalone; proves there are buyers for flow-metrics forecasting, at a premium price.
- **Not verified:** piplanning.io's capacity and holiday handling; Ativo and Jira Align capacity details.

## Market and adoption

- **State of Agile (Digital.ai, 18th report, October 2025):** about 350 respondents, mostly coaches and consultants at large enterprises; vendor-run, so directional only.
  - SAFe at 44% among scaling approaches (26% the year before; the figure swings a lot year to year).
  - 48% use a blended or hybrid scaling model and 26% a home-grown framework.
  - Only 27% say agile is enabling them to deliver value.
  - 44% rely on spreadsheets for half or more of their insights.
  - Supports positioning on predictability and on quarterly planning beyond strict SAFe.
- **Atlassian:** more than 350,000 customers; 55,369 with over $10,000 in Cloud ARR (Q2 FY2026); FY2026 Cloud revenue $4.4B.
- **Jira Premium** (required for Jira Plans and its Program board): about $14.5–$18.3 per user per month, depending on tier and billing.

## Platform signals

- **Rovo:** extra usage billed from December 3, 2026. Atlassian charging for AI usage may slow Rovo adoption in planning in the short term, but capacity-aware planning by Rovo is a likely threat within 12–24 months.
- **Trust signals enterprises look for:** "Runs on Atlassian", Cloud Fortified (apply once eligible), a security and architecture sheet, a DPA template, a SOC 2 readiness roadmap.

## Opportunities to time

- **Miro renewals in 2027,** after the acquisition closes: content on "keep your PI planning in Jira", factual, not fear-based.
- **Atlassian's own Program board** is documented as unsuitable for big-room sessions: Atlassian Community answers on that exact question are a natural entry point.

## Other strategies considered

- **Coaches and consultants as a channel:** many run their own Jira sites or work in client sandboxes where they can install the Reality Check; its findings, handed to a client, put OV in front of decision makers. Worth testing during the beta.
- **A data-health report as a separate offer, later:** a new CIO or PMO head in their first 100 days may value an objective audit of planning hygiene even without PI planning.
- **Integrate with Atlassian Goals and Focus** for the OKR layer rather than duplicating them.

## The business, honestly assessed

- Enterprise buying is slow: Jira admins test, budget is approved, procurement and legal may follow (often 3–9 months for a first deal). "Runs on Atlassian" and the Atlassian invoice shorten it; works councils can lengthen it (`docs/engineering/privacy.md`).
- ServiceNow costs before it pays (partner program, certification, security review). Later, only when a customer pays for it.
- Crowded space (Competitors above). The edge must be the method (planning against measured probabilities, data health, OKR/hypothesis gates), not data access.
- **Desirability:** PI planning in Jira is a felt pain (Atlassian, Miro, Scaled Agile and several vendors all address it), but revealed demand for dedicated apps is modest (hundreds of installs for the best-rated one).
- **Market:** Atlassian reports more than 55,000 customers with over $10,000 in Cloud ARR; perhaps 14,000–19,000 of them run multi-team quarterly or PI planning (estimate). The realistic paying pool for a new entrant within three years is in the low hundreds.
- **Scenarios at month 36** (modelled estimates, not forecasts):
  - Pessimistic: about 8 paying customers, about $40k ARR.
  - Base: 30–40 paying customers, about $180k–$300k ARR; cash break-even in year 2.
  - Optimistic: about 100 paying customers, about $700k–$1M ARR.
- **Odds (a judgment, not a measure):** the full vision profitable within 6–12 months of launch, under 5%; Jira only, covering its costs in the mid term, about 25–35%.
- The buyer of the outcome layer is whoever pays for waste (CFO, CEO, transformation office), not whoever approved the features.
